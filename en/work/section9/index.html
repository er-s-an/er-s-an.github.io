An evaluation harness for agents that fix agents.

Section9 connects runtime evidence, code, controlled execution, and business acceptance in one incident-response workflow. I built a paired showcase that compares single-agent and multi-agent runs without hiding failures or assuming that more agents win.

Evaluation built around business outcomes

The harness pairs a single-agent baseline with a multi-agent arm under the same task specification. It records task status, wall-clock latency, token usage, unknown outcomes, and source identity instead of reducing success to a persuasive transcript.

Prompt, cost, loop, and composite failure scenarios each completed one paired closed-loop run on the final source version. The resulting eight arm runs preserve both successes and earlier failures.

Controlled repair, not unbounded autonomy

Section9 combines scoped grants, leases, fencing, cancellation, independent acceptance, and immutable run identities. A repair is evaluated against the affected business behavior after execution, while late responses and stale authority cannot quietly overwrite a newer run.

The read-only showcase projects real task events into an inspectable office and evidence drawers. The interface explains the system, but the underlying events and acceptance reports remain the source of truth.

Evidence that survives scrutiny

The final composite pair passed business acceptance on both arms: the single-agent baseline took 25.007 seconds and 5,338 tokens; the multi-agent arm took 31.905 seconds and 7,183 tokens. This is a recorded result, not a claim of multi-agent superiority.

OpenTelemetry and Langfuse traces, browser acceptance, source hashes, and raw run artifacts make the experiment inspectable end to end.

Evaluation setup

Four controlled scenarios have one normal pair each on the final source version. These are small, fixed-fixture experiments with remote model inference; they do not establish statistical stability or a general advantage for either arm.
